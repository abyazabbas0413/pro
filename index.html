Paper consumption
in the digital age.

Paper is useful — for learning, packaging, records and creativity. But every sheet has a story behind it: resources, water, energy, transport and waste. The smart goal is use less when possible, reuse what you can, and recycle correctly.

Environmental impacts

The hidden cost of paper

Think of paper as a chain. The impact starts before the page reaches your desk and continues after disposal.

🌲
Forests & fibreVirgin paper uses wood fibre. Recycled fibre can reduce demand for new raw material.
💧
Water & processingPulping, bleaching and papermaking use water and industrial processes.
⚡
Energy & emissionsManufacturing, drying, converting and transport all require energy.
🗑️
WastePaper that is not reused or recycled can end up in landfills or other waste pathways.

Paper isn't the villain.

The better question is: what job does the paper need to do? A physical copy can be valuable for learning, accessibility or official records. For routine drafts, internal sharing and announcements, digital workflows can often reduce unnecessary printing.

Best rule: Reduce first → Reuse → Recycle.

Smarter alternatives

Digital doesn't mean waste-free

Digital tools use energy and hardware too. Choose them where they genuinely replace unnecessary paper.

01 · Documents

Digital-first workflow

  • Share PDFs instead of printing drafts.
  • Use comments and version history.
  • Print only the final or required copy.
02 · Learning

Study smarter

  • Annotate digitally for routine reading.
  • Print difficult diagrams when useful.
  • Use double-sided printing for notes.
03 · Reuse

Give paper a second life

  • Reuse one-sided sheets as rough paper.
  • Flatten and sort cardboard correctly.
  • Follow your local recycling rules.
